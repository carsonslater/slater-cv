I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in the statistical science department at Baylor University writing to apply to the Graduate Data Science Intern position with Dell Technologies Services in Round Rock, TX. As I finish my Ph.D., I am looking for an opportunity to apply the statistical methods and programming skills I have developed through my coursework, consulting, and industry work, and this internship seems like the ideal place to continue growing as a data scientist.

I believe that I am a viable candidate for this role to advance the mission of Dell technologies. In my current job, I have been able to apply my skills to large-scale, real-world data. I built and assessed generalized linear models as pricing models on datasets exceeding 100 GB to improve an auto insurance rating plan, all within the regulatory requirements of the Texas Department of Insurance. I also ingested and evaluated new vendor data to determine whether purchasing it would be profitable, assessing over 200 potential new rating variables. On top of that, I work with millions of policy rows to answer ad-hoc business questions for stakeholders. This work has taught me to move quickly between deep technical modeling and practical, decision-ready answers for people outside of statistics.

Before this role, I worked on contract projects with Denver Water to develop methods for understanding single-family residential customer water usage. It involved developing data processing pipelines in R, combining different types of data pertaining to customer usage and customer features, and crafting tailor-made solutions and dashboards to assess and inform Denver Water's residential conservation efforts. I was able to uncover that roughly 20\% of the Denver Water customer base does not irrigate their outdoor spaces, which led them to reconsider their conservation strategy to focus more on targeted messaging. I also consulted for the American Holistic Nursing Association, where I used a mixed-effects model for longitudinal survey data to assess patient improvement under different treatment frameworks. In that project I collaborated with graduate students as peers and presented our model findings in a parsimonious fashion to the stakeholders.

This internship demands an individual who can work on projects ranging from basic, effective methods to cutting-edge experimental ones, and who can explain the results to business stakeholders. Whether it is learning to scrape weather data for modeling purposes, or learning proper prior specification on a covariance matrix for a Bayesian model, I am able to get into the weeds on both sides of the coin and communicate those concepts to technical collaborators and nontechnical stakeholders alike. I thrive in environments where I am working on multiple projects at the same time, and I am comfortable collaborating across teams and adapting to the needs of different stakeholders. My background has equipped me to translate complex modeling into objective, actionable insights.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my background makes me a great fit for this role.
